# Synthetic vs Real Linguistic Comparison

## Purpose

This analysis addresses potential circular bias concerns by demonstrating that synthetic transcripts generated by GPT-4.1 differ systematically from real clinical conversations. These differences justify why Experiment 5 (real data) provides the primary validation evidence, while Experiment 1 (synthetic data) serves only as a controlled pilot.

## Sample Characteristics

- **Synthetic (GPT-4.1)**: 50 transcripts
- **Real (DoVA)**: 50 transcripts
- **Simulated (Nature)**: 50 transcripts

## Key Findings

### Statistically Significant Differences

The following features differ significantly across transcript sources:

| Feature | Synthetic | Real (DoVA) | Simulated (Nature) | p-value |
|---------|-----------|-------------|---------------------|----------|
| Word Count | 548.66 | 2730.56 | 1306.08 | 0.0000 *** |
| Turn Count | 40.32 | 291.12 | 98.44 | 0.0000 *** |
| Type Token Ratio | 0.52 | 0.28 | 0.36 | 0.0000 *** |
| Filler Rate | 5.51 | 6.39 | 8.00 | 0.0000 *** |
| Medical Jargon Rate | 0.55 | 1.14 | 1.48 | 0.0000 *** |
| Avg Sentence Length | 6.72 | 6.99 | 9.32 | 0.0000 *** |

*Significance: * p<0.05, ** p<0.01, *** p<0.001*

## Interpretation

### Why These Differences Matter

1. **Synthetic transcripts are shorter**: GPT-4.1 generates more concise conversations than real clinical dialogues.

### Implications for Validation

These systematic differences mean:

- **Experiment 1 results may not generalize**: Scoring performance on synthetic transcripts likely overestimates reliability on real clinical conversations.

- **Circular bias concern is real**: Using LLM-generated targets to validate LLM scoring creates artificial consistency.

- **Experiment 5 provides primary evidence**: The 250 real transcripts with 20 assessments each (5,000 grading attempts) offer genuine validation without circular bias.

## Conclusion

Experiment 1 should be interpreted as a **controlled pilot** demonstrating that the rubric structure is interpretable by LLMs in ideal conditions. However, validation claims must rest on Experiment 5's real-data evidence, which shows reliability holds despite the linguistic complexity of natural clinical conversations.
